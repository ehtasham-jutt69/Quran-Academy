What is Noorani Qaida and Why is it the Essential First Step?

Noorani Qaida (نوراني قاعدة) is the universally acclaimed syllabus formulated to teach non-Arabic speakers how to correctly read the Holy Quran. Originating in India and refined across centuries of Islamic scholarship, it breaks down the complex phonetic architecture of classical Arabic into digestible, incremental building blocks.

Many students who attempt to dive straight into the Quran without mastering Noorani Qaida struggle with persistent stumbling, mispronouncing similar-sounding letters (such as ح vs ه, or ع vs أ), and failing to apply fundamental Tajweed rules. By completing Noorani Qaida first, a student develops vocal muscle memory and phonetic intuition, allowing them to read any Ayah of the Holy Mushaf fluently without hesitation.

Complete 17-Lesson Noorani Qaida Curriculum

1
Single Arabic Letters (Mufradat)

Mastering individual letters (Alif to Yaa) with their exact throat, tongue, and lip articulation points.

2
Compound Letters (Murakkabat)

Recognizing how Arabic letters join together in initial, medial, and final cursive script forms.

3
Abbreviated Letters (Muqatta'at)

Pronouncing mystical opening letters found at the beginning of Surahs (Alif-Lam-Meem, Yaa-Seen).

4
Short Vowels (Harakat)

Fatha (Zabar), Kasra (Zair), and Damma (Pesh). Learning short, crisp vowel sounds without stretching.

5
Double Vowels (Tanween)

Two Fathas (Do Zabar), Two Kasras, and Two Dammas producing the soft nasal 'N' ending sound.

6
Harakat & Tanween Exercises

Comprehensive word-building drills combining short vowels and Tanween for quick reading speed.

7
Standing Vowels (Khari Harakat)

Khari Zabar, Khari Zair, and Ulta Pesh—prolonging sounds for exactly one Alif duration.

8
Soft Vowels (Huroof Leen)

Waw Leen and Yaa Leen preceded by Fatha, creating smooth diphthongs (such as Khawf, Bayt).

9
Consonant Stop (Sukoon / Jazm)

Resting consonants and connecting sounds between syllables without breathing breaks.

10-17
Tashdeed, Madd & Waqf (Pausing)

Doubled consonants (Shaddah), long elongation (Madd), Idgham, and stopping rules when finishing verses.

Understanding Makharij: The 5 Primary Organs of Articulation

In Arabic, changing the point of articulation can completely alter the meaning of a word. For example, قَلْب (Qalb) with a deep throat Qaf means "heart", whereas كَلْب (Kalb) with a soft Kaf means "dog". Our certified Al-Azhar instructors give continuous live audio-visual correction to ensure students articulate each sound accurately:

  • Al-Jawf (The Oral Cavity): Three elongated vowel sounds (Alif, Waw, Yaa).
  • Al-Halq (The Throat): Six throat letters (Hamzah, Haa, Ayn, Haa, Ghayn, Khaa).
  • Al-Lisan (The Tongue): 18 letters categorized into deep tongue, middle tongue, tongue sides, and tip.
  • Ash-Shafatayn (The Lips): Four letters formed by lips (Baa, Meem, Waw, Faa).
  • Al-Khayshoom (The Nasal Cavity): Ghunnah nasalization for Noon and Meem.
